Brotherhood & Campus Life

Retreats, families, intramurals, cultural programming, interchapter trips — the parts of the fraternity that don’t fit on a résumé.

The big brother programme

You don’t join alone

Every new member is paired with a big brother — an active who has been through the process and whose job is to make sure you get through it too.

That pairing is not ceremonial. Your big is the person you call about a course you’re failing, an internship application you’re stuck on, or a decision you don’t want to make alone. Over time those pairings accumulate into family lines, and the family is what most brothers actually mean when they say “my brothers”.

Beyond the chapter

A network you can actually travel on

ΛΦΕ has chapters across six regions of North America, plus associate chapters including McMaster. Interchapter visits are a real part of the calendar — brothers from Alpha Xi regularly host and get hosted, and delegates go to the international convention each summer.

The practical version: you can land in a city where you know nobody and find someone who will meet you for dinner.

On campus

Part of the wider community

Alpha Xi works alongside U of T’s Asian cultural organisations and the broader Greek council rather than apart from them. Lunar New Year events, Asian Heritage Month programming, collaborative philanthropy, and joint socials are all standing fixtures.

We are Asian-interest, not Asian-exclusive — and the programming reflects both halves of that.
